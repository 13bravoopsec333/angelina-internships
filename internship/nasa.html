📝 Description

2,000+ students annually. Materials science, engineering, and research projects. STEM Gateway application portal. Short-term project-based learning.

✅ Eligibility

US citizen, 3.0+ GPA, currently enrolled in college

📋 Application Process

Apply through intern.nasa.gov (NASA's centralized portal). Requires: resume, unofficial transcript, statement of objectives, and 1-3 short supplemental essays. Two application cycles per year (fall and spring deadlines, typically ~6 months before start). After application submission, NASA center coordinators match students to projects based on skills/interests. Interviews depend on the specific center/project — some centers interview, others match directly. Security clearance process may be required for some centers.

🚀 Best Way to Apply

Apply through intern.nasa.gov — this is the ONLY official portal for NASA OSTEM. Also check Handshake for specific center postings. NASA has 10+ field centers — apply to multiple locations. For Materials Engineering: NASA Goddard (materials testing), NASA Glenn (materials & manufacturing), NASA Marshall (materials science), NASA JPL (materials for spacecraft). UF has strong NASA connections through the Florida Space Grant Consortium — contact them for guidance. Also check NASA Pathways program for recent graduates.

📄 Resume Tips

Lead with: Materials Science/Engineering major, GPA 3.96 (NASA values GPA highly), polymer research experience (Sumerlin group), NMR/FTIR/TLC analytical techniques, Creo/Solidworks CAD, Ansys Granta materials selection. Highlight any space-related coursework or interests. NASA values: technical skills, teamwork, communication, problem-solving. Mention Gator Blacksmithing (metalworking + leadership as Secretary), Material Advantage (materials professional society). For statement of objectives: be specific about which NASA center and what type of materials work interests you (composite materials for spacecraft, thermal protection systems, additive manufacturing for space, etc.).

🎯 Maximize Your Chances

1) Apply to MULTIPLE NASA centers — don't put all eggs in one basket. 2) Your statement of objectives should name specific NASA projects or research areas. 3) Contact the NASA OSTEM coordinator at your target center directly — email addresses are on the center's STEM engagement page. 4) Apply through Florida Space Grant Consortium as well for additional opportunities. 5) The application cycle is early — start preparing materials in fall for summer internships. 6) NASA values diversity — mention any unique perspectives you bring. 7) Follow up after applying with a brief email to the center's internship coordinator. 8) Security clearance: apply to centers that don't require clearance first for faster processing.

🎤 Interview Format

Varies by center — typically phone or video interview (20-45 min) with the project mentor/advisor. Questions: Why NASA? What specific research interests you? What skills do you bring? Describe your research experience. May include technical questions related to the specific project. Behavioral questions about teamwork and working in a diverse environment. Some centers skip the interview entirely if the mentor selects based on application alone.

🏆 Success Stories

NASA OSTEM is competitive but well-documented on Reddit (r/NASA, r/EngineeringStudents). Successful applicants report: strong GPA (3.5+), relevant research experience, specific knowledge of NASA projects/missions, tailored statements of objectives. Students who mention specific NASA research papers or mission needs in their essays stand out. Many successful interns found positions through faculty connections to NASA researchers. Reddit users emphasize that applying to less popular centers (Glenn, Stennis, Marshall) increases chances vs. JPL or JSC. Former interns describe the interview as relaxed and conversational — focused on shared research interests.

📝 Supplemental Questions

Statement of Objectives: explaining your interest in NASA, specific research areas, and career goals. Typically 500-1000 words. Some centers ask additional questions about availability, security clearance status, and citizenship. The supplemental essays are the most important part — they determine mentor matching.
