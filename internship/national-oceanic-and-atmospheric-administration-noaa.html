📝 Description

Two-year scholarship with academic assistance (up to $9,500/year) plus a 10-week paid summer internship at a NOAA facility. Strong for environmental engineering and materials-related research.

✅ Eligibility

US citizens, enrolled undergrads in STEM fields

🚀 Best Way to Apply

Apply through NOAA Education portal (noaa.gov/education/opportunities) for Hollings/EPP scholarships; for general internships use USAJOBS.gov. Some programs like the Hollings Scholarship require university nomination (UF's Office of National Scholarships coordinates this). Apply early — Hollings deadline is typically January/February for the following year.

📄 Resume Tips

Highlight any earth/environmental science coursework alongside materials engineering. NOAA values interdisciplinary skills. Emphasize: polymer research (chain polymerization from Sumerlin group), CAD skills (Creo/Solidworks for modeling ocean equipment), and analytical skills (NMR, FTIR). If applying to Hollings, tie materials science to ocean observing systems or marine technology.

🎯 Maximize Your Chances

Get UF nomination for Hollings Scholarship (campus deadline usually early fall). Network with NOAA scientists at AGU or Ocean Sciences meetings. Tailor application to NOAA mission areas: climate, oceans, weather, fisheries. UF's proximity to NOAA's Atlantic Oceanographic and Meteorological Laboratory in Miami is a plus. Follow up with NOAA Education Office after applying.

🎤 Interview Format

Phone or video interview, typically 30-45 minutes. Technical questions about your research experience and how it connects to NOAA's mission. May include behavioral questions (STAR format). For Hollings, there may be a faculty recommendation component.

🏆 Success Stories

Hollings scholars typically receive $9,500/year academic assistance + $700/week for a 10-week summer internship at a NOAA facility. Many go on to permanent NOAA positions. UF has historically had strong Hollings nominees.
