📝 Description

Packaging and engineered products engineering internship. Innovative solutions across various disciplines including engineering, supply chain, and manufacturing.

✅ Eligibility

Engineering students

📋 Application Process

1) Online application with resume, transcript, and brief cover letter. 2) HR recruiter phone screen (20 min). 3) Technical phone screen or video call with engineering manager (30-45 min). 4) On-site interview day at Evansville HQ or plant location (includes facility tour, team lunch, panel interviews with 3-4 engineers). 5) Offer within 1-2 weeks. Berry's process is relatively fast compared to large corporations.

🚀 Best Way to Apply

Apply through Berry's Workday portal — they consolidate applications internally. Evansville, IN is the headquarters and has the most intern positions. Attend any career fairs where Berry recruits (they target Midwest and Southeast engineering schools). If Amcor has acquired Berry by your application time, check both portals. LinkedIn outreach to Berry engineers in plastics/packaging can help get your resume flagged.

📄 Resume Tips

Emphasize plastics, polymers, and materials science coursework — your Materials Engineering major is a perfect fit for Berry. Highlight Creo and SolidWorks CAD skills (Berry uses both). Your NMR and FTIR experience from Sumerlin lab is relevant to polymer characterization. Mention any injection molding, extrusion, or blow molding exposure. Include GPA. Use action verbs: designed, optimized, tested, analyzed. Keep to one page.

🎯 Maximize Your Chances

Berry makes packaging for consumer goods, food, healthcare, and industrial — mention which sector excites you most. Your polymer research experience (chain polymerization) is directly applicable to plastics engineering. Berry values interns who understand sustainability — mention interest in recycled plastics or sustainable packaging. If you've used JMP for statistical analysis, mention it — Berry uses statistical process control. Apply early; Berry's smaller intern cohort fills quickly.

🎤 Interview Format

Two rounds: (1) HR phone screen with behavioral questions and logistics (start date, GPA, work authorization). (2) Technical video or on-site interview with engineering team — questions about materials science fundamentals (polymer properties, testing methods), CAD proficiency, and problem-solving scenarios. Berry asks practical questions like 'How would you troubleshoot a defective packaging run?' Behavioral questions focus on teamwork and initiative. Less formal than big-tech style interviews.

🏆 Success Stories

Berry interns work on real production problems — common projects include material substitution for cost reduction, packaging design optimization, and process efficiency improvements. Conversion rate to full-time is strong (50-60%). The Evansville location offers affordable cost of living. Past interns have become process engineers, materials engineers, and R&D specialists. Berry's flat organization means interns get significant exposure to senior leadership.

📝 Supplemental Questions

Common supplemental questions: 'What plastics or polymers have you worked with?', 'Describe a project where you used CAD software.', 'Why are you interested in the packaging industry?', 'What experience do you have with material testing or characterization?', 'Describe your career goals in engineering.' Some applications ask about willingness to work in manufacturing environments and travel between plant locations.
